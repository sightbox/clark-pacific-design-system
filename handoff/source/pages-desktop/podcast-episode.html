Episode Summary

When Kieran Timberlake was designing the UCSF Tidelands Student Housing project, they faced a challenge familiar to many architecture firms: delivering a high-performance, architecturally ambitious building on a compressed schedule with a demanding budget. Their answer? Prefabricated building systems — their first time working with the approach at this scale.

In this episode, host and Clark Pacific Chief Strategy Officer interviews Eli Allen about what led the firm to prefabrication, how the design-assist process worked in practice, and what surprised them most about the factory-built envelope.

"The precision we got from the factory was something we couldn't have achieved in the field. And the schedule — being able to run the facade parallel to the structure — completely changed what was possible."

Eli Allen — Principal, Kieran Timberlake
Topics Covered
Design-assist collaboration process from concept through construction
How factory tolerances affect architectural detail decisions
What architects need to know before specifying prefab systems
Sustainability benefits of off-site manufacturing for envelope
Schedule compression — how parallel fabrication changes project delivery
The future of prefabrication in high-design architecture
